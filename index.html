01 / Explore the universities

Different paths. Different possibilities.

A law degree normally follows a bachelor's degree. Accelerated 3+3 programs can potentially reduce the combined time, but they require careful course planning and separate law-school admission.

Carbondale · 3+3 option

Southern Illinois University

SIU Carbondale → Simmons Law

Political Science is among SIU's accelerated 3+3 majors. Eligible students may enter law school after completing qualifying undergraduate requirements.

Consider it forStudying and earning a J.D. in Carbondale; a potential shorter combined pathway.
Ask an advisorCan her transfer credits, major sequence and GPA meet 3+3 eligibility? Law admission is not automatic.

Springfield → Chicago · 3+3 option

University of Illinois Springfield

UIS undergraduate study → UIC Law

Political Science or Legal Studies connects classroom learning with government in Illinois's capital. Explore UIS's accelerated partnership with UIC Law.

Consider it forLegislative and policy exposure in Springfield; potential internships and an accelerated route.
Ask an advisorWhich credits transfer, what is the accelerated sequence, and when would she need to relocate? UIS itself does not award a J.D.

Urbana-Champaign · Traditional option

University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

Bachelor's degree → Illinois College of Law

Complete a bachelor's in a field that interests you, then apply to Illinois Law's three-year J.D. program.

Consider it forUndergraduate-major flexibility and studying in Champaign-Urbana.
Admissions contextIllinois Law is selective; compare your GPA, LSAT preparation, costs and financial aid against published admissions data.

The Springfield connection

Learn legislation by working in it.

UIS administers the Illinois Legislative Staff Internship Program, a paid, ten-month postgraduate experience with Illinois legislative offices.

Published stipend: $3,300/month (verify current terms when applying). A bachelor's degree is required.

This could fit between completing a bachelor's and starting law school, though it would generally add time to the overall plan.

04 / Questions worth asking

Bring these to an advisor.

Will all 40 credits transfer?

Which count toward the major versus electives?

Can a transfer student enter the 3+3?

What courses, GPA, LSAT and deadlines apply?

What would it actually cost?

Compare undergraduate tuition, J.D. tuition, fees, aid and relocation costs.

What if elected office comes first?

Ask about internships, pre-law advising, campaign/public-sector experience and flexible sequencing.
